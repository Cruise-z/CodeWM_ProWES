## Domain Reasoning

- Media metadata is a pure domain concept (title, duration, tracks) and should be represented by immutable value objects validated at construction.
- Playlist navigation is deterministic state over a list of media items: next, previous, jump-to-index; boundary handling is critical.
- Playback state transitions (stopped, playing, paused) must follow clear rules and side effects on elapsed position and clock binding.
- Seek operations must clamp to [0, duration]; invalid inputs produce errors or safe no-ops per API contract.
- Volume and mute are orthogonal to playback; volume is clamped [0.0, 1.0], mute is a boolean, effective volume respects mute.
- Deterministic headless clock must be abstracted: a tick-driven engine consuming a provided clock interface; tests simulate time without sleeping.
- Runtime/UI boundaries (filesystem, decoding, rendering) are excluded; we simulate playback timeline only.
- Errors and validation are part of domain; exceptions must be clear and not leak runtime details.

## Domain Expectations

- A Media object exposes id, title, duration_seconds (positive float), and optional metadata; construction validates duration > 0.
- A Playlist maintains ordered Media items, current index, and supports next(), previous(), and select(index) with bounds handling.
- A Player exposes play(), pause(), stop(), seek(seconds), and reports position_seconds, state, and current media.
- Position advances only when state is playing and the clock ticks; paused and stopped do not advance.
- Seeking clamps within [0, duration]; seeking beyond triggers clamping to bounds.
- Stop resets position to 0 and clears playing state; play resumes from current position if media exists.
- Volume set/get and mute toggle affect effective output level but do not affect clock or position.
- When playback reaches media duration, state transitions to stopped and position clamps to duration.

## User Stories

- As a viewer, I want to start, pause, and stop playback of the current video to control my viewing.
- As a viewer, I want to navigate the playlist (next, previous, and select a specific item) while respecting bounds.
- As a viewer, I want to seek to a specific time within the current video and not exceed its duration.
- As a viewer, I want to adjust volume and toggle mute to manage audio output.
- As a developer, I want a deterministic clock to simulate playback progression in tests without real time.

## Competitive Analysis

- VLC Media Player: full-featured playback with extensive codec support and playlists; complex runtime.
- mpv: powerful command-line player with scripting; advanced features beyond our scope.
- ffplay (FFmpeg): minimal player using SDL; largely runtime-focused and depends on native libraries.
- Simple Python media controller examples: often tightly coupled to UI and not easily testable.
